## The challenge

Halcyon Health runs 14 physiotherapy clinics across Manchester and the north of England. Most were independent practices before they joined the group, and each one arrived with its own way of booking patients. Two kept a paper diary at the front desk. Several shared a spreadsheet between receptionists. All of them called patients back.

The booking was never the expensive part. The missed appointment was. A patient would book a course of six sessions, miss the third, and the clinic would find out when the physiotherapist walked into an empty treatment room. At that notice the slot could not be filled, and the patient often never rebooked at all.

"Every clinic had its own way of doing this, and every way involved somebody's memory," said Dr. Amara Whitfield, Operations Director at Halcyon Health.

Reminders existed in the form of phone calls from the front desk. They reached the patients who were near a phone during clinic hours, which is not the same group as the patients who had an appointment.

## What changed

Halcyon put all 14 clinics on one Openslot workspace on the Team plan. The first patient booked through it 2 days after signup, and the rollout across every site, front desk training included, took the same 2 days.

Each clinic runs its own booking pages, one per appointment type: initial assessment, follow-up, and post-operative review. The pages differ where the clinics differ, and nothing else has to.

Availability rules replaced the knowledge that used to sit behind the desk. Working hours are set per weekday for each site. Every appointment carries a buffer afterward for notes. Each physiotherapist has a daily limit, and no page accepts a booking inside the next two hours.

Calendar sync connects the calendar each physiotherapist already used, so training days, travel between sites and leave stop being bookable without anyone copying them anywhere.

Then the part that moved the number. Every appointment page sends an email confirmation when the booking is made, a text the evening before, and a text an hour ahead. Every one of those messages carries a reschedule link and a cancel link.

"A reschedule link costs us nothing," Whitfield said. "An empty appointment costs us the slot and, quite often, the patient."

## The results

No-shows across the group fell 38 percent. The pattern matches what Openslot later found across [1.2 million bookings](/blog/what-1-2-million-bookings-taught-us-about-no-shows): the reminder arrives where the patient actually is, and the link gives them somewhere to go other than silence.

Cancellations now arrive a day or two ahead instead of turning up as an empty room, which is enough notice to offer the hour to someone on the waiting list.

Running 14 clinics in one workspace also changed what the group can see. Appointment types, buffers and reminder timing are set centrally, and each site still manages its own hours. When a clinic opens on a Saturday, that is one change in one place.

## What's next

Halcyon is testing routing forms for new patients: one question about the injury, one about the nearest site, and the patient lands on the right clinic's page rather than in a phone queue.

The group is also looking at workflows, so that a finished course of treatment sends a follow-up invitation six weeks later without a receptionist keeping a list of names.
