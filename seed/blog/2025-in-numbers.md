At the end of each year we write down what it looked like in numbers, mostly so that we remember it correctly. This is the note for 2025. It is short, because the numbers are the point.

## Bookings

4.1 million bookings were scheduled through Openslot in 2025. That is every intro call, interview, physiotherapy appointment, tutoring session and demo that went through a booking page, a Team page or an embed, and landed in two calendars instead of a thread of messages. Most were between two people who had never met, which is the case the product was built for: a first call, a first appointment, a first interview.

Behind each one is a link that somebody sent instead of asking "what works for you?" We think about that a lot. It is the whole product.

## Teams and countries

38,000 teams now use Openslot, in 140 countries. The average time from signing up to the first booking is 2 days, which is the number we watch most closely. It tells us whether the product explains itself, and whether the first page someone builds is one they are willing to send.

Some of those teams are one person with one page. Some are the ones you can read about on [the customers page](/customers):

- [Halcyon Health](/customers/halcyon-health) put 14 physiotherapy clinics on one workspace, rolled it out in 2 days, and cut no-shows by 38 percent with SMS reminders and reschedule links.
- [Orbital Recruiting](/customers/orbital-recruiting), an agency of 60 recruiters placing engineers, moved interview scheduling to round-robin pages and took time to first interview from 6 days to 1.5 days. They now run 2,400 interviews a month through Openslot.
- [Lumen Tutoring](/customers/lumen-tutoring) embedded booking on the profiles of 900 tutors. Students book 21,000 sessions a month there, and Lumen's support tickets about scheduling fell 70 percent.

## No-shows

In June we published [what 1.2 million bookings taught us about no-shows](/blog/what-1-2-million-bookings-taught-us-about-no-shows). The finding held for the rest of the year: 19 percent of bookings with no reminder ended in a no-show, and 11 percent of bookings with an email reminder, an SMS reminder and a reschedule link in the message did. That finding is why SMS reminders shipped in July, and why the reschedule link is in every message by default. The reminder is only half of it. The reschedule link is the other half, because the people who cannot make it need a way to say so that takes one tap.

## Reliability and what shipped

Uptime for 2025 was 99.98 percent. A booking page that is down is a link that does not work at the exact moment someone decided to book, so we hold ourselves to this number and publish it. Most of the year's reliability work went into the availability engine, which Tomasz described in [why we rebuilt availability](/blog/why-we-rebuilt-availability).

The releases, in order: Team pages, SMS reminders, the rebuilt availability engine, embeds, and workflows and webhooks. Each has a post on [the blog](/blog) that says what it is, who it is for and how to turn it on.

## The team

Openslot is 12 people across 6 countries. We are remote, we meet through booking pages like everyone else, and we have not grown faster than the product needed. Support is answered by the people who build the product. That is slower for us and better for you, and everyone doing it is on the [about page](/about).

Next year starts with meeting polls and a month grid you can use with a keyboard. We will write about both when they ship. Until then, thank you for booking through us. Every one of those 4.1 million was somebody's time, and we do not take that lightly.
