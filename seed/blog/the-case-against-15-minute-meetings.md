We removed the 15 minute option from our own booking pages in September. This post is the reasoning. It is not a rule for you, and Openslot will keep letting you create 15 minute event types. But if you have been booking them by default, I would like you to reconsider.

## Where the 15 minute meeting came from

The 15 minute slot is a promise to the other person: I will not take much of your day. It shows up first in sales, as the low-commitment intro call, and spreads from there to check-ins, syncs, quick questions and "a few minutes to align".

The promise is sincere. The math behind it is not. A meeting does not cost its duration. It costs its duration plus the time to prepare, the time to get into it, the time to write down what happened, and the piece of the hour on either side that is now too small to use for anything else. A 15 minute meeting at 10:15 takes the hour.

## What happens in 15 minutes

Watch a few. The first three minutes are hellos and a screen that will not share. The next five are context, because at least one person did not read the notes. That leaves seven minutes for the actual purpose, and the purpose was a decision.

Decisions do not take seven minutes. What happens instead is one of two things. The meeting runs over, into the buffer nobody set, and the next meeting starts late. Or it ends on time with "let's pick this up", and a second meeting gets booked. Two 15 minute meetings are not a 30 minute meeting. They are a 30 minute meeting plus a day of waiting and a second round of hellos.

The cases where 15 minutes is genuinely enough are the cases where a message would also have been enough.

## The cost is on the calendar, not in the room

The bigger problem is not any one meeting. It is what a calendar full of them does to the day.

Four 15 minute meetings spread across a morning leave no gap longer than 40 minutes. Nothing that needs concentration fits in 40 minutes, so the morning is lost to work that needed the morning. The person who accepted those four meetings believes they gave away an hour. They gave away four.

We see a version of this in how booking pages get used. A page that offers short slots gets them booked all over the day, because that is what a short slot invites. A page with a 30 minute event type and a 15 minute buffer after it has fewer places to put a booking, so the bookings cluster, and the gaps between them are gaps you can use. The second calendar is the one you want.

## What to book instead

For intro calls, 30 minutes with a form. Ask the two questions that decide whether the call should happen at all, and let the answers set the agenda. We wrote about this in [a better first call](/blog/a-better-first-call). A 30 minute call with an agenda ends early more often than a 15 minute call ends on time.

For recurring check-ins, 25 minutes on a 30 minute grid, with the buffer built in. The five minutes are for notes. [Buffer time is not optional](/blog/buffer-time-is-not-optional) has the reasoning.

For the quick question, no meeting. Write the question. If the answer needs a conversation, the reply will say so, and then book 30.

For decisions, 45 minutes and a written proposal sent the day before. Most of these end in 20.

## When 15 minutes is right

There are real cases. A scheduled hand-off where both people know exactly what is being handed over. A daily standup with a fixed format. A follow-up to confirm one thing that could not be confirmed in writing. In each of them the purpose is fixed in advance, nobody needs context, and nothing is being decided.

If your 15 minute meeting fits that description, keep it. If it does not, it is a 30 minute meeting that has been asked to apologize for itself. Give it the time, set a buffer after it, and let it end early.
