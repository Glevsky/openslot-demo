A no-show is the most expensive thing that can happen to a booking. The time was reserved, the preparation was done, and nothing came back. We wanted to know how often it happens and what actually changes the number, so we looked at 1.2 million bookings made through Openslot.

Two numbers came out of it. Without reminders, 19 percent of invitees did not show up. With an email reminder, an SMS reminder, and a reschedule link inside the message, 11 percent did not. Everything else in this post is context for those two numbers.

## How we counted

We looked at bookings that reached their scheduled time. Anything canceled or rescheduled beforehand was excluded, because a moved meeting is not a missed one. A booking counted as a no-show if the host marked it as missed afterward, or if the connected video tool reported that the invitee never joined. Bookings with no outcome recorded either way were left out rather than guessed at.

We grouped bookings by the reminder settings on the booking page at the time of the meeting: no reminders at all, email only, and email plus SMS with a reschedule link in every message. SMS reminders are in beta, so the last group is smaller and more recent than the others. It is also mostly made up of workspaces that came looking for SMS because they already had a no-show problem, which if anything works against the result.

This is not a controlled experiment. Workspaces that turn reminders on may differ from workspaces that do not in ways we cannot see. We are reporting what we observed, not what we proved.

## The headline

| Reminder setup | No-show rate |
| --- | --- |
| No reminders | 19 percent |
| Email and SMS, reschedule link in every message | 11 percent |

Email on its own moved the number, but most of the distance between the two rows came from adding SMS and, we think, from the reschedule link being in the message the invitee actually reads.

Nineteen percent means roughly one in five reserved slots produces nothing. Eleven percent is still one in nine, and nobody should read this post as a cure. But for a clinic with 200 appointments a week, the difference is about 16 rooms a week that would otherwise have sat empty.

## The reschedule link is not decoration

The pattern that surprised us most was where the no-shows went when they went away. In the reminder group, reschedules went up. Not cancellations. Reschedules.

That fits what people tell us about missing meetings: very few of them decided to skip. Something came up, the meeting was not the first thing on their mind, and there was no obvious way to say so. A reminder with a reschedule link gives them one, at the moment they are thinking about it, on the device they are holding.

A reminder without a way out is a nag. It tells the invitee they are about to be late and offers nothing to do about it. The link turns the reminder from an announcement into a question, and a rescheduled booking is one you still get to have.

## Other patterns

We saw a few more things that were consistent enough to mention, without putting a number on them yet.

- Lead time matters. Bookings made more than a couple of weeks ahead were missed more often than bookings made for the same week. The further away the meeting, the less real it feels.
- First meetings are missed more than repeat ones. An invitee who has met the host before shows up. Someone who booked through a link on a website and has never spoken to anyone is the person the reminder is for.
- Timing matters more than wording. Reminders sent the evening before did more than reminders sent the morning of, and a second reminder about an hour before the meeting helped again. We tested a handful of message texts and could not tell them apart.

We will publish more when we can say it with confidence.

## What to do this week

1. Turn on email reminders on every booking page. They are on every plan, including Free, and they are the floor.
2. Send two: the evening before, and about an hour before. Make sure the reschedule and cancel links are in both.
3. If your invitees are people who do not live in their inbox, patients, candidates, students, add SMS. SMS reminders are in beta on Pro and Team, and we are opening them to every paid workspace soon.
4. Shorten lead time. A date range of two or three weeks on the booking page keeps meetings close enough to feel real.
5. Treat a reschedule as a win. It is a meeting that would otherwise have been empty.

Halcyon Health, a group of 14 physiotherapy clinics in Manchester and the north of England, ran SMS reminders with reschedule links across all of their clinics during the beta and saw no-shows fall by 38 percent. Their story is on the [customers page](/customers/halcyon-health), and it is the same mechanism at work: the reminder arrives where the patient is, and the link gives them somewhere to go.
