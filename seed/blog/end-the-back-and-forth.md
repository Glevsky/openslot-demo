Before we started Openslot, I kept a tally for one month of how many messages it took to schedule a single sales call. The average was seven. Two to propose times, two to say those did not work, one to ask which time zone we were talking about, one to confirm, one to move it.

None of those messages were rude or slow. The back-and-forth was not a politeness problem. It was a process problem, and process problems have process fixes.

## Why the back-and-forth happens

Every scheduling email carries one piece of information the other person does not have: when you are free, how long you need, which time zone you are in, where the meeting will happen. Two calendars, neither visible to the other side, and a conversation that moves one fact at a time.

The fix is to put all of those facts in one place the other person can see, and let them choose. That is the whole idea behind a booking link. The rest of this guide is about doing it well, because a badly set up link causes a different kind of back-and-forth: the one where the invitee books a slot you did not really want to give away.

## Put your real availability in one place

Start with the calendar. Connect the one you actually live in, so that anything already on it is hidden from the booking page. If you keep a personal calendar with school runs and dentist appointments, connect that one too. A booking page that does not know about your Thursday afternoon commitments will hand them out.

Then set working hours per weekday. Not the hours you are theoretically at a desk, the hours you are willing to take a call. Most people who set 9 to 6 every day end up moving the 9:00 Monday and the 5:30 Friday within a week. Set the hours you would happily accept and leave everything else closed.

Two settings do more than they look like they should:

- Minimum notice. If you need a day to prepare for a call, require 24 hours. Same-day bookings are how you end up unprepared at 2:00 with a prospect you have not researched.
- Date range. Opening the next three weeks is plenty. Opening the next six months invites bookings you will forget about.

### Protect the time around the meeting

A booking page that is set up correctly will fill up. That is the point, and it is also the failure mode. Three more rules keep a full calendar from becoming an unworkable one:

- A buffer after every meeting, ten or fifteen minutes, so notes get written and the next call starts on time.
- A daily limit. Four external calls a day is a lot for most people. Set the number you can do well, not the number you can survive.
- A buffer before, if you need one, for meetings where you have to prepare.

These settings are unglamorous, and they are the difference between a booking link that helps and one that eats your week.

## Make the link do the explaining

Give each kind of meeting its own event type with a plain name and an honest length. A 30 min intro call. A 60 min demo. Names like "strategy session" tell the invitee nothing about what will happen or how long it will take, and they will ask.

Write two sentences in the description. What the call is for, and what the invitee should have ready. Set the location once, whether that is your video tool or a phone call to a number the invitee enters, so that nobody has to ask where you are meeting on the day.

If you run several kinds of meetings, resist the urge to put them all on one page. A prospect looking for an intro call does not need to see your 90-minute onboarding session. Send the link to the one event type that fits.

## Send the link without apologizing

There is a widespread feeling that sending someone a booking link is a little rude, as if you were asking them to do your admin. I understand it, and I think it is wrong when the link is sent well. What is rude is seven emails.

The message that works is short, offers an alternative, and does not pretend the link is a favor:

> Here is my calendar for the next couple of weeks. Pick whatever suits you, and if nothing does, tell me two times that work and I will make one of them free.

The alternative matters. It tells the other person the link is a convenience, not a wall. Almost nobody uses it. They pick a slot.

## Let the reminders do the follow-up

Once a booking exists, the last source of back-and-forth is the meeting itself moving. Someone gets sick, a flight changes, a priority shifts. Without a reschedule link, that is another three messages. With one, it is a click, and your calendar updates on its own.

Turn on email reminders for every event type and make sure each message carries a reschedule link and a cancel link. ## Checklist

- Connect every calendar that has commitments on it, personal ones included.
- Set working hours you would actually accept a call in, per weekday.
- Require enough notice to prepare, and open no more than a few weeks ahead.
- Add a buffer after every meeting and a daily limit.
- Create one event type per kind of meeting, with a plain name and an honest duration.
- Send the link with a short note and an alternative.
- Turn on email reminders with reschedule and cancel links.
