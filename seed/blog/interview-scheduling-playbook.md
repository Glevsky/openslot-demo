Every hiring team we talk to has the same bottleneck, and it is not sourcing. It is the days between "we would like to talk to you" and the first call. Candidates go quiet in that gap. Recruiters spend it chasing interviewers for free time.

This playbook is what we have seen work for teams that interview across time zones. It is built around the setup [Orbital Recruiting](/customers/orbital-recruiting) used to take time to first interview from 6 days to 1.5 days, and it applies whether you run 2,400 interviews a month, as they do, or 20.

## Why interviews are the hardest meetings to schedule

A sales call involves one host and one invitee. An interview loop involves a recruiter, one or more interviewers, a hiring manager and a candidate who has a day job, and each stage adds people. Every added calendar multiplies the ways a time can fail.

Remote teams make this worse in two ways. Interviewers sit in different zones, so the overlap window is small. And nobody sits near anyone, so the hallway question "are you free at three?" costs a message, a wait and a reply.

The fix is not to try harder at the back-and-forth. It is to remove it, one stage at a time.

## The playbook

### 1. One booking page per stage

Create a separate page for each stage in your loop: recruiter screen, technical interview, hiring manager conversation, panel. Each gets its own duration, its own people and its own rules.

The temptation is one generic "interview" page with a 45 minute default. Resist it. A 20 minute screen and a 90 minute panel have nothing in common except the word interview, and a single page forces rules that are wrong for both.

Name pages by role and stage, for example "Backend engineer, technical interview". Candidates see the name, and it tells them what to prepare for.

### 2. Round-robin for screens, collective for panels

This is the decision that moves the numbers most.

For stages where any qualified person can run the interview, use a round-robin Team page. Add every recruiter or interviewer who can take a screen. The candidate sees the union of their availability, so there is almost always a slot in the next day or two, and bookings rotate so nobody carries the load alone.

For stages where specific people must all attend, use a collective page. The candidate sees only the times when every named interviewer is free. That window is smaller, which is exactly why collective stages should be few and short.

Orbital's loop is a round-robin screen, a round-robin technical interview drawn from a pool of engineers, and one collective conversation with the hiring manager and a lead at the end. Before Openslot, time to first interview averaged 6 days. With round-robin pages across 60 recruiters, it is 1.5 days.

If you are not sure which type a stage needs, [round-robin vs collective](/blog/round-robin-vs-collective) walks through the trade-offs. Both page types are on the Team plan.

### 3. Protect the interviewers

An interview pool only works if people stay in it. Interviewers drop out when scheduling makes their week unmanageable, so set the rules that keep them willing.

- Daily limits. Two interviews a day is a common ceiling for engineers. Three technical interviews in one afternoon produce a bad third interview.
- Buffers. Put 15 minutes after every interview for notes. Feedback written in the same hour is worth more than feedback written the next morning, and a buffer is the only way to make it happen. [Buffer time is not optional](/blog/buffer-time-is-not-optional) explains what happens without it.
- Working hours per weekday. Let interviewers block a no-interview day. Deep work needs one.
- Minimum notice. 24 hours for interviews. Same-day interviews sound fast and feel rushed for everyone.

Set these per person first, then per page. The page rule is the floor; a person's own rule can only tighten it.

### 4. Reminders and reschedule links on every stage

Across 1.2 million bookings, the no-show rate was 19 percent with no reminder and 11 percent with an email reminder, an SMS reminder and a reschedule link in the message. Interviews follow the same pattern. Candidates rarely vanish on purpose. They forget, or the time stops working and they are embarrassed to say so.

Turn on email and SMS reminders for every stage, and keep the reschedule link in the message. A candidate who moves the interview themselves at 8 in the morning costs nobody anything. A candidate who does not show costs the interviewer an hour and the recruiter a day.

### 5. Measure time to first interview

Pick one number and watch it weekly: the median time from the moment a candidate is invited to schedule to the moment the first interview happens. Everything above moves this number, and it is the one candidates feel.

Keep the other measures simple. Interviews per interviewer per week tells you whether the pool is balanced. No-show rate per stage tells you whether reminders are reaching people. Neither needs a dashboard; a weekly export is enough.

## Checklist

- One booking page per interview stage, named by role and stage.
- Round-robin pages for screens and any stage with a pool.
- Collective pages only where specific people must all attend.
- Daily limits and buffers set for every interviewer.
- Minimum notice of 24 hours on interview pages.
- Email and SMS reminders with a reschedule link on every stage.
- Time to first interview tracked weekly.
