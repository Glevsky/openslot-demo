Every team that complains about meetings is working from an impression. Ask how many hours went to meetings last month and almost nobody can answer, which is why the conversation goes in circles and why the recurring invite nobody wants survives another quarter.

This workbook replaces the impression with a count. Four weeks of logging, an afternoon of adding up, and a decision on each meeting that you can defend to the person who scheduled it. Eighteen pages, most of them blank on purpose. The whole thing runs alongside normal work, at roughly ten minutes a week and one afternoon at the end of it.

The worked example runs on a team of nine who logged 214 meetings over four weeks and found that a third of the total hours sat in two recurring invites. The larger of the two was a standing update with no chair. Your numbers will differ, and the point is to have some.

## What's inside

- Four weekly log sheets, one line per meeting: who called it, how long, how many people, what it produced.
- Six categories that cover almost every meeting, so sorting takes minutes rather than an argument.
- The counting step, in two pages: hours per person per week, and the same figure per category.
- Four questions to put to every recurring meeting, including the one about what would happen if it stopped.
- A decision page for each meeting: keep, shorten, make async, or drop, with a reason written next to it.
- A worked example filled in end to end, so you can see what finished looks like before you start.
- A short script for proposing a cut to a meeting somebody else owns, without making it personal.
- A keep page for the meetings that survive, with the shortest agenda that still makes them work.
- A follow-up page to run the same count a quarter later and see whether anything drifted back.

## Who it's for

- Team leads who suspect the calendar is the problem and want evidence before saying so.
- Operations managers going into a planning cycle who need a number, not a feeling.
- Anyone who has tried a no-meeting day and watched it fill up again within a month.
