Once a year we look at what a full year of bookings says, and write down only the parts we can defend. This is the 2026 edition, and it covers 2025: 4.1 million bookings made through Openslot by 38,000 teams across 140 countries.

It describes what happened rather than what will happen next. Every number comes from bookings we can see, so it is a picture of teams who already schedule with a link. Where that skews a result, the page says so next to the chart rather than in a footnote at the back.

The short version is that the settings with the largest effect are the ones most teams skip. Reminders are the clearest case. Bookings with no reminder attached were missed at 19 percent. Bookings with an email reminder, an SMS reminder and a reschedule link inside the message were missed at 11 percent. Nothing else we measured moved a number that far, and turning reminders on takes about a minute.

## What's inside

- The year in totals: 4.1 million bookings, 38,000 teams, 140 countries, and 99.98 percent uptime.
- No-show rates by reminder setup, with the 19 and 11 percent figures and the method behind each.
- Time from signup to first booking, which averaged two days, and what the slower teams had in common.
- How team pages get used in practice: round-robin for volume, collective for panels, and the point where each one stops helping.
- Availability rules ranked by how often teams set them against how much they change the result.
- Meeting length across the year, including how much of a working week goes to meetings of 15 minutes or less.
- Five charts you can reuse internally, each with its counting method printed next to it.
- A closing section on what we could not measure, and what we would need in order to.

## Who it's for

- Operations and RevOps leads who have to explain where the meeting time goes.
- Sales managers deciding how inbound calls should be split across a team.
- Anyone who has been asked for a benchmark and would rather cite a method than a headline.
