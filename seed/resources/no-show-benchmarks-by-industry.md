The question we get after publishing a no-show number is always the same: is that us. An overall rate of 19 percent is not much help to a clinic manager with 200 appointments a week, and it is no help at all to a tutoring marketplace where every session is booked by a different person. An average is a fact about everybody and a description of nobody.

So we cut the same 1.2 million bookings by sector and wrote down what each one looks like on its own. Healthcare, education, recruiting, professional services and consumer services, each with its own distribution rather than a single figure.

Two warnings, both repeated inside. This is observational data from teams who already use booking links, so it describes them and not a whole market. And the spread within a sector is wider than the distance between sectors, which is the most useful finding in the report. We would rather you left with a range you trust than a single figure you can quote.

## What's inside

- How the 1.2 million bookings divide across the five sectors, and how many workspaces sit behind each.
- Where each sector falls relative to the 19 percent overall rate, with the range as well as the middle.
- Which sectors move furthest toward the 11 percent reminder figure, and which barely move at all.
- First appointments against repeat ones, cut by sector, which explains most of the gaps between them.
- Lead time by sector, and why the sectors that book furthest ahead carry the highest rates.
- A healthcare reference point: the 38 percent fall in no-shows at a group of 14 physiotherapy clinics.
- The counting method in full, so you can run the same cut on your own booking data and compare fairly.
- What a sector average is good for, and the three ways we have watched one get misused.
- A one-page summary written to be put in front of people who will not read 22 pages.

## Who it's for

- Clinic and practice operations leads who need a comparison rather than an average.
- Education and tutoring teams booking sessions at volume across many instructors.
- Analysts who want to see the method before they quote the headline.
