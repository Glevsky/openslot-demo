Most hiring problems get blamed on sourcing. The gap that actually loses candidates sits later, between the message that says you would like to talk and the first call that happens. Candidates go quiet in that gap, and recruiters spend it chasing interviewers for free time.

This is the chapter on closing it. Twenty-six pages on scheduling alone, with no advice about job ads, scorecards or offers. It assumes your interviewers sit in different time zones and that none of them will answer a calendar question inside an hour. It also assumes you cannot add a person to fix it, which is where most of the advice comes from.

The reference setup throughout is the one a 60-recruiter agency in Berlin used to take time to first interview from 6 days to 1.5 days while running 2,400 interviews a month. The same structure works at twenty interviews a month, and where the agency setup is heavier than a smaller team needs, the page says which parts to drop first.

## What's inside

- One booking page per stage: recruiter screen, technical interview, hiring manager call, panel.
- Round-robin screens, with load rules so the same two people do not absorb the whole week.
- Collective pages for panels, and what to do when the overlap window is only two hours wide.
- Time zones. What the candidate sees, and the confirmation wording that removes the follow-up question.
- Interviewer protection: buffers, daily limits and minimum notice that survive a busy hiring month.
- Candidate reminders and reschedule links, written for someone who is interviewing around a day job.
- Time to first interview: a definition, a weekly measurement, and the two things that usually move it.
- Rescheduling inside a loop, so one moved interview does not push the other three out with it.
- What to do when a candidate goes quiet after booking, and the point at which to stop chasing.
- A worked example with the full settings from the agency setup, ready to copy stage by stage.

## Who it's for

- In-house recruiters running loops across more than one time zone.
- Agency teams scheduling on behalf of clients who do not share a calendar with them.
- Hiring managers who would rather be booked than consulted about every slot.
