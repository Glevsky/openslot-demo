A first call goes well or badly in the first two minutes, and that is decided before anyone joins. If you already know what the other person is trying to fix, the half hour is theirs. If you do not, ten minutes of it go on finding out, and those ten minutes are never given back.

These six agendas each cover one kind of first call. Every one comes with three questions for the booking form, a meeting name that says what will actually happen, the confirmation to send, and the follow-up to write afterwards. The three questions are the part that does most of the work, and the rest follows from them.

They are free and there is nothing to fill in. Take the closest one, put it in your own words, and drop any question you could have answered yourself before the call.

## What's inside

- Inbound demo request. 30 minutes with someone who found you and already has a problem in mind, so the call opens in their words rather than yours.
- Outbound intro. 20 minutes with someone who did not ask for the call, where the aim is a second conversation or an honest no.
- Renewal conversation. 30 minutes with a customer, held a month before the renewal date rather than the week after it.
- Partner first call. 30 minutes to find out whether there is anything worth building together, and what each side would have to do first.
- Technical qualification. 45 minutes with the person who will actually run the thing, including the questions they will want to ask you.
- Handover call. 15 minutes to move an account to a new owner without making the customer repeat two years of history.

## Who it's for

- Founders taking their own first calls who want them shorter and more useful to the person on the other side.
- Sales teams where every rep runs the first call a different way, and the notes afterwards show it clearly enough.
- Anyone whose calls open with ten minutes of context gathering that a booking form could have collected in advance.
