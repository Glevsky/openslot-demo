An interview loop falls apart in the gaps. The screen is booked within a day, then the technical stage waits four days for two calendars to agree, and by the time the final conversation happens the candidate has an offer somewhere else. Nobody made a bad decision along the way. The loop just had no shape, and every handover cost a day.

These four templates cover a loop from first stage to decision. They assume round-robin pages, so a stage is booked with whichever interviewer is free rather than with one named person, and availability rules that stop an interviewer losing a whole Tuesday to a panel.

Orbital Recruiting runs a version of this across 60 recruiters and went from 6 days to 1.5 days to first interview. Round-robin and collective team pages are on the Team plan, which is set out on [pricing](/pricing). The templates themselves are free and there is no form.

## What's inside

- Stage plan. One table with the four stages, the duration of each, who is allowed to take them, the buffer afterwards, and the maximum per interviewer per day. Fill in the names once and the loop is defined.
- Interviewer availability. The rules each interviewer copies once: working hours, 15 minute buffers, a cap of three interviews a day, 12 hours minimum notice, and a date range for people who only interview in some weeks.
- Candidate messages. The booking page description, the confirmation and the reminder, each carrying a reschedule link, what the stage covers, and the name and role of whoever the candidate will meet.
- Debrief. A collective page for the panel, booked at the same time as the final stage, so the decision has a slot on the calendar while the detail is still fresh.

## Who it's for

- Recruiters and coordinators running more than five loops a month across several teams at once, often with the same interviewers.
- Hiring managers who keep turning into the bottleneck for one stage of every loop.
- Small teams with no coordinator, where interviewers book their own stages and nobody owns the gaps between them.
