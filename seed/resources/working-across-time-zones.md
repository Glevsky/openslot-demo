Most distributed teams do not have a time zone problem. They have a fairness problem. There is one overlap that works for everybody, one person who keeps taking the call at 6am, and a week that nobody wants to look at on Monday morning.

This is a live session, 45 minutes, on 22 April 2026. Tomasz Wierzbicki has rebuilt time zone handling at Openslot twice, and he will go through the arithmetic of overlap, the settings that hold it in place, and the two cases that break most schedules: a country that moves its clocks on a different weekend, and a teammate who travels.

Sofia Moreau joins from Lumen Tutoring, where 900 tutors take bookings from students who are rarely in their zone. Ten minutes at the end are kept for questions. Register and the recording arrives afterwards, whether or not you make the live hour.

## What we cover

- Working out the honest overlap between two, three and four regions, and what to do when there is none.
- Working hours per weekday, so nothing can be booked before or after the hours a person actually works, including the short day at the end of the week.
- Minimum notice and daily limits as a defense against the 6am booking that every other setting technically allowed.
- Rotating the uncomfortable slot across a team with a round-robin page, instead of volunteering the same person every time it comes up.
- What the invitee sees, which is their own zone, always, including the weeks when offsets change in one country and not the other.
- Reading a shared team calendar across zones without doing arithmetic in your head, and what to put in the meeting name so nobody else has to either.
- Booking pages for people who travel, so a month in another country does not quietly turn into a month of early calls.

## The speakers

- Tomasz Wierzbicki, CTO and co-founder, Openslot. Writes the code that decides which slots exist, and has shipped the bugs to prove it.
- Sofia Moreau, Co-founder, Lumen Tutoring. Runs a marketplace where 900 tutors and their students are almost never in the same zone.

## Who it's for

- Operations and people leads who schedule across regions and would rather have a rule than a habit.
- Support and engineering teams on a rotation that crosses at least three zones and rolls every week.
- Anyone who has apologized for a meeting invitation in the last month, or moved the same call twice.
