An empty appointment slot is not a small problem in a clinic. The room is staffed, the practitioner is there, and the patient who would have taken that hour is three weeks down the waiting list. Reminders are the usual answer, and most clinics already send them without seeing the rate move. Sending more of them is rarely the fix.

In this recorded session Mara Lindqvist talks to Dr. Amara Whitfield about what worked at Halcyon Health, a group of 14 physiotherapy clinics in Manchester and the north of England. All 14 went onto one workspace in two days, and no-shows fell by 38 percent after SMS reminders with reschedule links went out. The waiting list at each clinic is why that number matters, and it is where the session starts.

Thirty-eight minutes, with the settings on screen and the parts that did not work left in. Two of the changes made no measurable difference, and Dr. Whitfield explains why they were kept for a month anyway. If you run more than one site and each does things slightly differently, the middle section is the one to watch.

## What we cover

- The rollout: 14 clinics on one workspace in two days, and which decisions had to be made first.
- Availability that matches a clinic: hours per practitioner, buffers between appointments, daily limits.
- SMS reminders with a reschedule link in every message, and the 38 percent fall that followed.
- What goes in the message and what Halcyon decided to leave out of it.
- Filling the freed slot: what happens when a Thursday afternoon comes back the day before.
- Rolling out to sites that already do things their own way, without flattening the differences.
- The front desk: which questions stopped arriving once patients could move their own appointment.
