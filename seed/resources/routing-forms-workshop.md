A routing form asks a question first and sends the invitee to the right booking page. That is the whole idea, and it goes wrong in ways that only surface three weeks later: a rule that catches everything, a question nobody outside the company can answer, a page with nobody behind it.

In this recorded workshop Priya Raman builds one from an empty page while Mara Lindqvist argues with the questions. The build is unedited, including the two points where the logic had to be pulled apart and written again, and the part where a test answer lands on the wrong page.

Routing forms are on the Team plan, alongside round-robin and collective pages and meeting polls. The workshop runs 55 minutes, and if you want to follow along in your own workspace the 14-day Team trial needs no card.

## What we cover

- Choosing the two or three questions that genuinely sort people, and dropping every question that only adds friction.
- Writing rules in order, and why the first matching rule should always be the narrowest one you have.
- Sending an answer to a single booking page, a round-robin team page or a collective page, and when each of the three is right.
- The fallback route, so an answer you did not plan for lands somewhere with an owner instead of nowhere.
- Hidden fields that carry what you already know from the page the invitee came from, so nobody is asked the same thing twice.
- Routing someone to a page that has no free times left, and what they should see when that happens.
- Testing with real answers before the form goes on a live page, and the three numbers worth watching in the first week.

## The speakers

- Priya Raman, Head of Product, Openslot. Builds the form on screen and explains every rule as it goes in, including the ones she takes back out.
- Mara Lindqvist, CEO and co-founder, Openslot. Takes the invitee's side and asks what each question is for and who will read the answer.

## Who it's for

- Revenue and marketing teams routing inbound traffic to the right seller without a manual triage step in between.
- Operations people who own the forms on a website and the booking pages sitting behind them.
- Anyone with a routing form already running that sends too many people to the wrong place.
