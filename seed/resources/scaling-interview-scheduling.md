Hiring teams usually meet the scheduling problem twice. First when a recruiter starts spending more of the day on calendars than on candidates, and again when the answer to that is to hire a coordinator whose job is to send the same messages faster. Neither point is a sourcing problem, and neither one is solved by working harder at the messages.

In this recorded session Jonah Adebayo talks to Felix Brandt about the other route. Orbital Recruiting places engineers from Berlin with 60 recruiters and runs 2,400 interviews a month, and took time to first interview from 6 days to 1.5 days without adding a coordinator to do it.

Forty-two minutes, including the last ten on questions from the audience. It is a working session rather than a talk, so the settings are on screen and you can copy them as they go past. There are almost no slides. Most of the time is spent inside a real workspace.

## What we cover

- Why adding a person to interview scheduling adds messages instead of removing them.
- One booking page per stage, and the settings that differ between a screen and a panel.
- Round-robin screens across 60 recruiters, with the load rules that keep the week even.
- Collective pages for panels, and what Orbital does when only two hours of the day overlap.
- Candidate reminders and reschedule links, written for someone interviewing around a job.
- Rescheduling inside a loop, so one moved interview does not push the other three out with it.
- What stays with a person: offers, difficult conversations, and anything needing judgment.
- Time to first interview: how Felix measures it every week and what he does when it rises.

## The speakers

- Jonah Adebayo, Head of Growth at Openslot. Wrote the no-show study and the interview scheduling playbook.
- Felix Brandt, Head of Talent at Orbital Recruiting. Runs interview scheduling for a 60-recruiter agency in Berlin.

## Who it's for

- Talent leads hiring across more than one time zone.
- Recruiting coordinators who want the calendar work to shrink rather than move.
- Hiring managers who would rather be booked directly than asked about every slot.
- Operations leads being asked to approve a coordinator hire this quarter.
